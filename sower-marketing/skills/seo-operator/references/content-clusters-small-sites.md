# Content clusters for a new site with little content

*Open this file when you plan a cluster on a new or content-light site: the order of its pieces, how they link, and when a second cluster may start. Such a site has nothing to arrange yet, and its risk is publishing volume before there is anything real to say.*

**Grades.** **D** = read in the named source, on the date its evidence row gives; **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence. Where each line comes from: `evidence.md`, its section on this file.

## The order, for a site with no content yet

1. **Ship the thing the content would be about.** A blog about a tool that does not exist yet, or that no one has used, has no first-hand fact to draw on: every post would be generic by construction. The content follows real use, never the other way round.
2. **Collect real questions before you write answers.** Once there are real users, even a few, the questions they ask (in support requests, in a forum, in feedback) are the cluster's topic list. A cluster built on questions nobody asked is written to rank and not to answer, which is the purpose the search engine's policy on scaled content names; a handful of pages is not the scale that policy is about (I; `SKILL.md` section 1).
3. **One narrow topic first, not a content calendar.** Do not plan twelve topics in four categories before any of them exists. Pick the one topic with the most first-hand material behind it now, and finish its cluster before you start a second. Five to seven pieces, for example:
   - an overview;
   - a walkthrough for a beginner;
   - a comparison with the alternatives;
   - an edge case, or the fix for a common problem;
   - a deeper piece for someone who already knows the basics.
4. **Link from the first day.** When a piece is published it links to the pieces of the cluster that already exist, and those get their links back to it in the same step, not in a later pass. The first piece has nothing to link to yet, and that is no reason to hold it. Pages on one topic that do not link to each other are, to a reader and to a crawler, separate pages (I).
5. **A second cluster only once the first is real.** Real means: its pieces link to each other, answer questions people actually asked, and each carries a first-hand fact. A second thin cluster before the first is solid doubles the risk instead of spreading it.

## When blogging has just been declared a priority

A declared priority creates pressure to publish on a schedule, and that pressure produces the generic pattern this skill warns against. When the schedule and the first-hand fact conflict, say so plainly and offer the smallest real alternative: one post now, built on a fact already in hand, instead of a calendar of posts made to fill dates. Nothing measured says a slower cadence wins; the reason is this skill's own rule that a post needs a first-hand fact (I; `SKILL.md` section 2).

## Community content is a different surface

A forum, the comments under a leaderboard, or any stream of posts by users is not measured or gated by this file's standard of clusters and first-hand facts. Its risks are spam, moderation load and abuse, not thin content. Treat it as a separate problem with its own tools: rate limits, a moderation queue, a way to report a post.
