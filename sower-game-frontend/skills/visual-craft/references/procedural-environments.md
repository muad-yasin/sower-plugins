# Generated and procedural environments

*Open this file before you generate or review any procedural environment or asset. The generator's side of the same discipline, its gates, output invariants and instrumentation, belongs to whoever owns the generator code; the two are one workflow.*

**Grades.** **Seen** = it happened on the source project, with its record named in `evidence.md`. **D** = documented in a named source, read on the date given. **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence.

- **Write acceptance criteria before generating, judged at the size the player actually sees.** State the size on screen, what must be legible at that size, and what may collapse into texture. On the source project a city map's buildings, drawn one per cell of a fine grid, read as a woven texture, because the map's window on a phone was only about 1080 by 420 and the whole 3072 by 3072 canvas was squeezed into it. Drawing the blocks at the coarser scale of the avenues fixed it, and on 18 July 2026 the window became a clipped view that zooms to each delivery's route (Seen, July 2026). **The palette is part of the criteria:** name the allowed colours and the one permitted accent before generating, and reject drift of the palette like any failure of composition (I).
- **The five tells of a structurally generic environment, each a question to put to the result:** (1) a **perfect geometric silhouette**, a filled square or diamond instead of an irregular boundary; (2) **no geography**, nothing the layout has to answer to, no river, coast or hill; (3) **invisible hierarchy**, main roads indistinguishable from side streets; (4) **uniform distribution**, parks and landmarks sprinkled evenly like confetti instead of clustered; (5) **one material family everywhere**, no districts. The named failure is **the generic carpet**: strong detail in every block and no identity as a place. On the source project a regenerated, bigger city map showed all five, and its owner rejected it as generic and unlike a real city (Seen, July 2026). The record is that one map, with all five together: it does not show that one tell alone is a reason to reject (I). Reject on the criteria written before generating, in the view the player has. **Detail at the level of blocks cannot rescue a structure that is generic.**
- **Budget hand-placed landmarks.** Require a few deliberately placed anchor elements that do not repeat (a river bend, one big park, one odd building), with the generator filling around them, and at least one hand-made quirk per shipped asset (I: the source project's earlier skill states it). The review question, asked directly: *would a player believe a conscious hand placed this?* The source project's rework placed its parks as anchored clusters, one big and two medium with a small sprinkle, instead of an even confetti (Seen).
- **Judge every iteration at two framings,** at full size and in a crop at the zoom the player sees: the source project's rework judged each of about ten bakes that way (Seen). Make the generator log its risky features' positions in coordinates a review can use, so that a crop goes straight to them (I: the earlier skill's rule).
- **When quality gates cut a feature's count below the target, prefer fewer correct instances to a looser gate:** chasing the count brings back the broken instances the gate rejects (I). Record which knobs would raise the count later, so that the compromise stays reversible: on the source project its owner accepted 2 bridges on that seed, quality over count, and the river's meander amplitude and a pocket threshold were recorded as the knobs (Seen).
- **Frame bake and capture cameras on the content's own vertices projected through the actual camera, never on a bounding box in world space.** Under a yawed or isometric camera a bounding box is a poor proxy for the extent on screen: framing on a box's corners left about 40% of a city bake empty under a 40-degree yaw (Seen).
- **Instrument before iterating.** When you tune a threshold or a gate against observed output, first add cheap instrumentation of the decision path, counts of rejections per gate and a sampled distribution of the real data, and tune against the numbers. The named failure is **blind threshold roulette:** on the source project three blind attempts to tune the bridges failed before a log of rejection counts showed one curvature gate rejecting 93% of the candidates (Seen).
