# Colour accessibility: the checkable half

*Open this file before a palette decision that touches text or the interface, before a contrast or colour-blindness check, and before any accessibility audit. `SKILL.md` keeps the hard floor, that colour never carries meaning alone, and `ux-design` holds the thresholds; this file makes the rest computable instead of judged by eye.*

**Grades.** **Seen** = it happened on the source project, with its record named in `evidence.md`. **D** = documented in a named source, read on the date given. **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence.

## Contrast is a formula, not an impression

- **The ratio is exact:** (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 and L2 are the relative luminances of the lighter and the darker colour, and "Contrast ratios can range from 1 to 21". Relative luminance weights the linearized sRGB channels as 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B (D, WCAG 2.2, the W3C Recommendation of 12 December 2024, its definitions, read 2026-11-22). Green weighs most because the eye is most sensitive to it (I).
- **The thresholds are `ux-design`'s,** from the same Recommendation. Why 4.5:1: 3:1 is the minimum the ISO and ANSI standards recommend for normal observers, and acuity of 20/40 goes with a loss of contrast sensitivity of about 1.5, so a user with 20/40 needs 3 times 1.5, or 4.5:1; and 20/40 "is commonly reported as typical visual acuity of elders at roughly age 80" (D, W3C, "Understanding Success Criterion 1.4.3: Contrast (Minimum)", read 2026-11-22). Any audience can include older players (I).
- **WCAG 3 is a draft, and its contrast measure is open:** its Working Draft of 10 September 2026 says that the contrast algorithm "is yet to be determined" (D, read 2026-11-22). So claim compliance against the 2.2 ratio. A newer perceptual contrast model can be a second instrument when you judge a dark interface, never the ground of a compliance claim (I).
- **Compute against what reaches the screen** (`SKILL.md`). A stored value is that only for an opaque pair: a check on the stored value of a translucent or blended colour inherits, in Unity, the linear lift of `unity.md`, "Colour space is the first thing to establish". **There, take the two colours from a real capture.** For a web page's conformance W3C points to the browser or the styles and away from the text as shown on screen, because antialiasing renders a thin glyph fainter than its colour (D, the same page): a capture is sampled off the flat of a glyph for that reason (I).

## Colour-blindness proofing: the simulator's model matters

- **The scale:** "About 1 in 12 men have color vision deficiency", and the most common type makes red and green hard to tell apart (D, the National Eye Institute, "Color Blindness", read 2026-11-22).
- **A check in a simulator is only as good as the simulator's model.** The model of Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes handles normal colour vision, anomalous trichromacy and dichromacy in one way (D, "A Physiologically-based Model for Simulation of Color Vision Deficiency", IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 2009, its authors' page read 2026-11-22), so prefer a simulator built on it for the anomalous types (I). Viénot's model of 1999 and Brettel's of 1997 are older models for the full dichromacies, Brettel's the usual one for tritanopia, and a simple colour-matrix filter proves little (not checked at a source; stated here since September 2026).
- **The protocol:** run the rendered capture, ideally the contact sheet so that the whole set is checked at once, through a simulator with a good model, and confirm that every colour-coded *pair of states* survives; profit green against loss red first in anything with an economy (I). **Where a pair does not survive, the fix is the hard floor: add the cue that is not colour (a sign, an arrow, a shape, a label), not palette surgery.** On the source project the green and red trade numbers of its broker app were always paired with plus and minus signs or arrows (Seen). The check is that every *new* colour-coded state keeps to that.

## Palette ratio and naming the harmony

- **60/30/10,** one dominant colour, one supporting and one accent, is a working ratio that keeps a small reused palette from fragmenting as assets accumulate: the quantified form of "variation from value and saturation, not from more hues" (I: the source project's earlier skill states it, and no case was found).
- **Name the harmony system the palette actually is** (analogous with an accent, split-complementary, triadic with one dominant colour). Not for theory's sake: a named system gives every proposed addition a test, whether it extends the system or only the list of swatches. An unnamed palette can be defended only by taste, which is the argument that loses to "but it looked nice on this one prop" (I).
