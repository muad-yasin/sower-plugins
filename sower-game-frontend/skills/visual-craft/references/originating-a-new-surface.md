# Originating a new surface: the three generic default looks

*Open this file before you originate a genuinely new visual surface: a new screen background, a new room, a new family of props with no precedent. A reskin or an extension of an existing anchor does not need it: the anchoring rule of `SKILL.md` covers that case, because there is something to extend.*

**Grades.** **Seen** = it happened on the source project, with its record named in `evidence.md`. **D** = documented in a named source, read on the date given. **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence.

## Where there is no precedent to anchor to, know what the default looks like before you choose

A genuinely new surface has nothing to extend, and that is where a generated design converges on whatever the current generic look is (I). A public skills repository lists the looks that AI-generated pages currently cluster on, among them the three below, and calls them defaults, not choices (D, anthropics/skills, `skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md` at commit 2c5958ec, lines 34 to 45, read 2026-11-12 in a research round of ours). The three, each with its tell:

- **(a) The broadsheet:** hairline rules, zero corner radius, dense newspaper columns, a high-contrast serif. A product built around news, articles or documents is pulled toward it; the source project, a game built on fictional news outlets, was (I: its earlier skill says so).
- **(b) Dark fintech:** a near-black ground with one acid-green or vermilion accent. It is the cliché of real trading apps (I), and so the *last* look a satire of one should arrive at by accident.
- **(c) Warm cream with a serif and a clay accent:** one of the looks AI-generated design clustered on at the time of that read, and a tell in its own right.

**A deliberately chosen dark foundation is not failure (b).** The difference is whether it was chosen and recorded, and whether its accents are the product's own or the genre's. On the source project the dark system was a recorded decision, near-black surfaces on a four-step ladder with two font families (Seen). Where such a system exists, a new surface **extends** it through the anchoring rule instead of deriving "dark trading app" from the genre again, and the two produce different results.

Each of the three is right for some brief. **None is a choice when it arrives unexamined.** If a new surface lands on one, say that you recognised it and why it is still right here, or move away from it: the same bar as for a new palette colour, a stated reason and not a feeling.

## The siblings: an interface, and a generated environment

These three are looks: a palette, a type treatment and a layout, read from a list about generated pages. For a room or a family of props, what applies of them is the two palettes; the broadsheet is a layout and a type treatment (I). An interface screen or layout has its own signature, purple-to-cyan gradients, frosted-glass panels, grids of identical cards and a bounce on every transition, covered in `../../ux-design/references/new-visual-directions.md`: the same failure on a different surface. The structure of a generated environment has its own tells: open `procedural-environments.md` before you generate or review a map or a district.
