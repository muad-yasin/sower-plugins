# Budgets, the craft ladder, and AAA and indie pipelines

*Open this file before authoring a new class of asset and before judging a tooling proposal: the frame and triangle bands, materials as batch boundaries, texel density, decimation, LODs under a fixed camera, the craft ladder, and how AAA and indie pipelines differ.*

**Grades.** **Seen** = it happened on the source project, with its record named in `evidence.md`. **D** = documented in a named source, read on the date given. **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence.

## Budgets: the numbers an asset is authored against

These turn "keep it lean" into checks that can fail.

- **The frame.** A frame lasts about 33.3 ms at 30 fps and 16.7 ms at 60. For mobile, Unity's advice is to leave about 35% of each frame idle, so the chips can cool and the battery lasts: about 22 ms per frame at 30 fps, and about 11 ms at 60, which it calls difficult on many devices (D, Unity, "Best practices for profiling game performance", read 2026-11-22). Everything below fits under that.
- **Triangles per mesh.** Unity's current manual gives no band: the count "depends on the visual quality you require and the platform you are targeting", fewer polygons run faster and more give finer shape (D, Unity 6000.6 manual, "Creating models for optimal performance", read 2026-11-22). So the band to author against is a measured one. On the source project each figure was 1,420 to 1,480 triangles, at 44 x 93 pixels on screen in one room and 91 x 244 in another (Seen). Spend the top of your band deliberately, on the assets closest to the player, and keep props well under it, so that the hero spend does not compound; a prop that drifts towards the hero count needs a stated reason (I).
- **Scene tripwires.** Set limits for triangles on screen and for draw calls from a measurement on your lowest target device, and treat them as tripwires, not laws (I).
- **Materials are batch boundaries** (`SKILL.md`, "Budgets"). One shared flat-material recipe does at small scale the job a texture atlas does at AAA scale: it is the batching discipline, so protect it (I). The Unity specifics are in `unity.md`, "Batching".
- **Texel density barely matters for flat-colour materials**: there is almost nothing to mismatch (I). If a textured asset ships, state one density per class of asset and hold its neighbours to it, because mismatched density between neighbours shows even when each texture is fine on its own (I).
- **Decimation, when an imported or generated mesh is over budget.** Blender's Decimate modifier has three modes (D, Blender 5.3 manual, "Decimate Modifier", read 2026-11-22). Collapse merges vertices progressively, keeping a ratio of the faces (0.5 keeps half), with options for symmetry on one axis and a vertex group that steers it; Un-Subdivide removes the edges a subdivision added; Planar "reduces details on forms comprised of mainly flat surfaces" under an angle limit, and its delimits can keep material borders, UV seams, sharp edges and the edges of a UV map. The manual adds that the modifier is not usually for meshes modelled carefully and economically. So: Planar, with its delimits on, for hard-surface and architectural pieces; Collapse for organic shapes, where nothing but a vertex group protects a material border or a seam, so it suits background props and meshes not yet unwrapped and is never a finishing move on a hero asset (I). Afterwards, merge by distance, recalculate the normals, and check the faces for flipped orientation (I).
- **LODs under a fixed camera** are the source project's reasoned non-goal (`SKILL.md`, "Budgets"): LOD serves a varying view distance, and a fixed camera shows every asset at one known size. **If a camera gains real distance variation, the decision reopens with it**, as that project's earlier skill says (Seen).

## The craft ladder: what better judgement looks like in this work

Career-ladder descriptions of 3D asset work, mostly from recruiting and education sites, describe a progression in judgement over constraints rather than in modelling speed (not checked at a source; stated here since September 2026). This file's reading of it, with the source project's cases:

- **Junior: executes defined tasks correctly**: retopology, UVs, a prop to spec. Here that means following the loop as written, with every check actually run (I).
- **Senior: budgets against real constraints and sets the technical standard.** The source project's two cases (Seen): rigid single-bone skinning chosen on purpose, because no organic deformation is visible at those sizes and it is safer at the 90° hip flex every seated figure holds, where smooth automatic weights everywhere is the default a junior reaches for; and the polygon budget decided against measured on-screen size, the rule of `SKILL.md`'s "Budgets".
- **Master: designs the pipeline itself**, seeing the recurring cost before it lands and building the structure that removes it. The source project's case (Seen): imported bone rotations do not fit a pose system of absolute local rotations, and "discard the bones, rebind the mesh" became the standing rule, which made a character change a mesh swap. For a new class of asset, that is the question to ask: what recurring cost does this create, and what one-time structure removes it (I)?
- On a solo project the identity that fits is the deliberate generalist technical artist, who owns the bridge between art and engine rather than depth in one modelling lane (I).

This ladder is about pipeline and technical judgement. Before judging how a result looks, open `../../visual-craft/references/critique-and-vlm-qa.md`; this file is for deciding how it gets built.

## AAA and indie pipelines: the landscape, and why a small shape is right

Worth knowing so that tooling proposals can be judged against it, not so that any of it gets adopted:

- **Modular reuse is the shared principle at every scale** (I). At AAA scale one form of it is the trim sheet: for *Sunset Overdrive*, a relatively small team of environment artists built a huge open world on a texturing technique of a set trim layout and normal map structure, with a UV mapping script and a shader for the texture style (D, Insomniac Games, "The Ultimate Trim: Texturing Techniques of Sunset Overdrive", GDC 2015, its GDC Vault session page read 2026-11-22). The small-scale counterpart already exists and costs less: a shared flat-material recipe, the rule of duplicating a verified shape, and one character template prefab. A trim-sheet pipeline proper is texture infrastructure a flat-colour game has no use for (I).
- **Where AAA diverges is procedural infrastructure.** For *Far Cry 5*, Ubisoft built procedural tools that generate biomes, texture the terrain, set up freshwater networks and generate cliff rocks across 100 square kilometres of wilderness whose terrain changed every day (D, "Procedural World Generation of 'Far Cry 5'", GDC 2018, its GDC Vault session page read 2026-11-22). Such tooling pays back at world-scale content volume, which a solo developer cannot and should not reproduce (I).
- **The indie counterpart of that proceduralism is narrow, scripted determinism**, and it is free (I). A committed generator script gives the property that matters, generation that is deterministic, re-runnable and diffable, without the infrastructure: the script is the source of truth and the scene and export files are regenerable artifacts, the same design as a procedural digital asset at solo scale.
- **Node graphs sit between the two, and adopting them is a decision with stated criteria, not a drift** (I). They earn their place for parametric variation at volume, many varied instances of one prop family, and even then scripting the same variation keeps everything in one diffable codebase. Two costs come with them: the modifiers have to be applied into the mesh at export, which Blender's FBX exporter can do (D, Blender 5.3 manual, "FBX (Legacy)", read 2026-11-22), since the engine receives only the result (I); and node graphs have changed incompatibly between major versions (not checked at a source; stated here since September 2026). Add the recall weakness of `llm-failure-modes.md`. Procedural mesh generation at run time is not worth it for a bounded set of assets (I).
