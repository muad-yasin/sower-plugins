# First session, onboarding and the store page

*Open this file before you scope an onboarding, a tutorial, a first-time experience or an early beat, and before you prepare a store listing. It rests on the source project's practice and on outside sources named where used; nothing here comes from testing with live users.*

**Grades.** **Seen** = it happened on the source project, with its record named in `evidence.md`. **D** = read in the named source on the date given. **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence. Where each line comes from: `evidence.md`, its section on this file.

- **Time to the core verb is the yardstick of the first session.** Stopwatch the fresh install yourself: the seconds and taps from launch to the first real use of the core verb, and from the first reward to the first real decision. Anything in that path, long dialogue or exposition that cannot be skipped, must earn its seconds. Write the limit into a guardrail so that it is a check, not an opinion: the source project's says that more than about 15 to 30 seconds to meaningful feedback on the fresh path is too slow (Seen).
- **One new system at a time.** Currency, then the core loop, then automation: one new kind of decision per beat, never two. A ladder of stages is the same principle at large scale; hold every new early beat to it at small scale.
- **Teach by doing, never by "press this button" modals.** A tutorial that locks the player to one allowed action is a named and repeated complaint about the genre (I: the source project's market research of August 2026, from players' own discussions). Give each new mechanic a real, hand-authored first instance, one concept per beat, never a tap-here overlay bolted onto an existing screen.

## The two overrides that earned it, and the fence around a third

The source project broke that rule on purpose twice, and the reasoning, not the precedent, is the fence.

**Override 1: the intro tutorial** (Seen, August 2026). Forced, unskippable, tap-gated to one allowed action and laid over the real screens: exactly what the bullet above bans. It stood because the difference is scope: the rule targets a lock-out imposed on the whole game, and this is a sequence of three to four minutes whose whole value is a guarantee of order. Review softened it twice, and each softening brought its own defect. Leaving the rest of the screen live let the player wander off, grind deliveries or outlast a timer; replacing the guaranteed first payout with an authored job let a fumbling first-timer earn less than the first purchase costs. Both were restored. The tutorial was later rebuilt from scratch, on the owner's call.

**Override 2: a guided walkthrough of one feature, mid-game** (Seen, August 2026). Four beats, about 45 to 60 seconds, over the real screens, replacing a full-screen pitch whose lines each described a screen the player could not see while it was being described. Three properties earned it, and they are the checklist for any future one: it is **consented** (it starts only from an explicit tap, never on its own), **skippable** (the hardware back button ends it cleanly, unlike the intro), and its failsafe is a **graceful dismiss, never a restart**: a progress reset in the middle of a game cannot ship, and the first-use highlights it hands back to are the designed fallback.

**A third guided sequence is a new argument made from scratch, never an inheritance from those two.** It is also the point, decided in advance, at which the intro's highlight geometry and its one clone are extracted into one helper: rule of three, settled before it is needed so that it is a search, not a debate (Seen).

## Onboarding runs over the first sessions, not the first one

Treat roughly the first week as onboarding (I): each early session hands the player a new system or a visible next goal. A ladder of stages already does this; so does an open loop, a goal in progress that the player wants to come back and finish, with an offline-earnings recap as the welcome back.

Ask whenever an early threshold moves: **at every likely quit point, is there a teased, legible next thing on screen?** A stretch with no new beat is the failure. The source project once found and closed exactly such a gap between two economy thresholds, with no new mechanic, unlock or tease in between (Seen). Check again after every retune: moving one threshold can reopen the gap without a sign.

## The store page

- **Google Play asks for 2 to 8 phone screenshots** (PNG or JPEG, each side between 320 and 3,840 pixels) **that show the actual game,** captured from it; tablets and Chromebooks take at least 4 if you add them, each side between 1,080 and 7,680 pixels (Play Console Help, "Add preview assets to showcase your app", read 22 November 2026, D).
- **Order the screenshots by what the game offers, not by when it happens:** the first core fantasy, then the breadth of the loop. The first ones carry the most weight, because a visitor may not scroll on (I).
- **A screenshot must read at thumbnail size the way a screen must read at arm's length.** The same discipline at a different distance.
- **Store-listing experiments are tests on live users under another name** (I): schedule them with the rest of external testing, not as a design step.
