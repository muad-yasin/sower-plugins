# Originating a new visual direction, and the two ways it goes generic

*Open this file before you propose a genuinely new screen, layout or visual direction. A reskin, a conformance pass or a milestone whose structure is locked does not need it: the direction already exists.*

**Grades.** **Seen** = it happened on the source project, with its record named in `evidence.md`. **D** = read in the named source on the date given. **I** = inferred, with the reason in the sentence. Where each line comes from: `evidence.md`, its section on this file.

## The generic look is a default, not a style

A model's first idea for a screen tends toward the average of what it has seen (I): purple-to-cyan gradients, frosted-glass panels, grids of identical cards with an icon, a heading and a line of text, a bounce on every transition. Grounding beats a cleverer prompt. Build every proposal from the project's frozen tokens (the real corner radii, the surface ladder, the type roles, the palette constants) and from what the live neighbouring screens actually render, never from what a genre looks like in the abstract. If part of that signature is already banned in your project, treat the rest as the same smell.

That is the signature of a screen. A new surface with no precedent, such as a background, a room's look or a family of props, has defaults of its own in `../../visual-craft/references/originating-a-new-surface.md`: three looks a generated design falls into, each with its tell. Before you generate or review an environment, such as a map or a district, open `../../visual-craft/references/procedural-environments.md` instead: the tells of a structure that is generic.

## Sketch two or three materially different directions before committing

For a genuinely new screen or layout, sketch two or three materially different directions first. In Nielsen Norman Group's study of parallel design, picking the best of four parallel designs and iterating on it measured 56% higher usability than the average of the four, and a design that merged the best ideas of all four measured 70% higher (Jakob Nielsen, "Parallel & Iterative Design + Competitive Testing = High Usability", 2011, read 22 November 2026, D). On the source project the owner picked from a three-way render, and not the option the plan had recommended (Seen, August 2026). Directions go to a review folder as mockups; decisions carry forward, the throwaway artifacts do not.

## Borrow a real app genre's reveal timing when the fiction supports it

Instead of inventing one. On the source project a job card showed only the guaranteed fee up front, and the rest came after the run (Seen); the reasoning was that real delivery apps order the same information that way, so the player already has the mental model (I). Where the fiction maps onto a real app genre, reuse its reveal timing instead of a bespoke one.

## A number on a result screen has to come from the result

On the source project the screen after a delivery repeated the customer's pre-rolled rating, the same number the offer card had shown before the run, with no connection to how the delivery went, until a real rating for each run replaced it (Seen). **A number shown where a result belongs is read as the result** (I): compute it from what just happened, or move it off that screen.
